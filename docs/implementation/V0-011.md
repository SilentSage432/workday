# V0-011 — Direct time selection

Date: 2026-10-02.

The user manipulates time before establishing what that time means.

V0-010 drew one civil day. This tranche lets the user point at a span of that day. The span is the time being referred to. It is not yet a fact.

## What was verified first

`main` was clean at `8e8faad42ce053d77b26f9f610bd18c8913026e1`, matching `origin/main`. Hosted migration history was still the seven files from `20261002213000` through `20261003040000`. Direct selection does not need a table, a column, or a migration. The selection is not domain truth, so it was not given one.

## Where it lives

`/schedule` still leads with the day canvas. `DayCanvas` holds the selection for the current civil day and discard token. `components/daySelection.ts` maps geometry and reduces the gesture. It does not import Timeline's composer or Supabase. `WorkSchedule` passes a discard token and does not store the range.

The selection value is `{ civilDate, startMinute, endMinute }`. Minutes are local clock minutes on that civil date, using the same 1440-minute clock face as the canvas. A pointer coordinate exists only while a gesture is in progress, so the scroll slop can be measured. After release, the idle gesture keeps no coordinates.

## Gesture

Pointer Events are the only input path.

Touch is the scroll conflict. The day still scrolls with `overflow-y-auto` and `touch-action: pan-y`. A touch that moves more than 10px before 220ms is a scroll: the hold is cancelled and the previous selection, if any, stays. A touch that rests for 220ms selects the 15-minute span under the finger, and a later drag adjusts it. A non-passive `touchmove` listener calls `preventDefault` only after selection has begun, so the rest of the drag does not scroll. If the browser cancels the pointer, the gesture is abandoned and the previous selection returns.

Mouse and pen select on press. They do not use the hold. A drag still orders the range. A pen that a device uses for scrolling is not given a separate hold in this tranche.

A tap selects 15 minutes. The hint on the canvas says so. The next gesture replaces the range. Clear removes it.

The all-day list is outside the timed surface. A press there does not select.

Timed facts use `pointer-events: none`. The surface receives the press where a fact is painted. The articles keep their accessible names. They are not draggable, resizable, or editable from the canvas. That leaves a later inspect gesture possible. It is not built here.

## Snapping

Fifteen minutes is the increment. The axis is 78rem for 24 clock hours, 3.25rem per hour, so one increment is 0.8125rem. Five minutes would be about 4px and would chatter under a finger. Thirty minutes would ignore planning increments the geometry can already distinguish. The hourly lines are unchanged.

A pointer ratio becomes a clock minute, snaps to the nearest 15, and clamps to 0 through 1440. Halfway rounds toward the later boundary. The smaller boundary is the start. If they are equal, the range grows by one increment, or back from the end of the clock if it is already there. An upward drag and a downward drag produce the same ordered range. The civil date does not change. The next midnight is the end, not the start of another day.

Rendering uses `startMinute / 1440` and the duration over 1440. The selection does not use the 1.75rem fact display floor, so a 15-minute band stays thin. The written range sits above the scroller: "6:00 PM – 9:00 PM". A range that runs from midnight to the next midnight adds "end of this civil day" so the two "12:00 AM" labels are not the whole sentence.

## What clears it

The session is keyed by civil day and a discard token. The token is the confirmed time zone, the canvas reload count, and a counter.

- Previous day, Next day, and Today change the civil day when they navigate, and the new session is empty. Today is only shown when the selected day is not the confirmed civil date.
- Opening Manage schedule increments the counter. Closing the tools does not put the range back.
- A successful Work-week save, and a successful Protected Time, Block, or Commitment save or remove, call `noteCanvasReload`, which reloads the day and increments the counter.
- Changing the confirmed time zone changes the token.

## Facts and appearance

The band is full width, `z-20`, a 15% stone fill, and a solid light top and bottom edge. It is not a wash, a hatch, a dashed border, or a card. It does not say available, free, open, or conflict. Facts stay painted. The band does not remove them.

Work, Protected Time, Blocks, and Commitments remain read-only on the canvas.

## DST

On a 24-hour civil day the clock check returns immediately. On a shorter or longer civil day, each selected 15-minute step is asked of the existing zone conversion. A minute that does not occur marks the range absent: "Part of this local clock range does not occur." A minute that also occurs one hour away marks it repeated: "Part of this local clock range occurs twice." The instant used for that question is discarded. The selection stays the local range. The sentences are not alerts and do not block the canvas. Creating a fact from this range later must validate the local time before anything is stored. This tranche does not create a fact.

## Accessibility and the phone

The range text includes "Selected time". Clear is a real button, "Clear selected time". The band is `aria-hidden` so it is not a second copy over the facts. There is no keyboard gesture that creates a range.

The layout target remains about 390×844: `max-w-full`, the existing `max-w-lg` shell, `overflow-x-hidden` on the frame, and the same bottom navigation. No horizontal control was added. Real touch on a signed-in phone was not available here. Pointer tests cover the gesture. The signed-in phone is still the user's check.

The scrollport does not auto-scroll during a drag. The pointer is clamped to the visible part of the axis. Time above or below that window waits until the user scrolls.

## Limits noticed while building

A 15-minute selection is a thin band. The sentence above the scroller carries the range. Dense lanes stay narrow, and the band tints them rather than resolving them. Touch still needs the short hold, or the day cannot scroll. A pen selects immediately. Dragging does not move the scrollport, so a long range that starts near the edge of the window has to be continued after a scroll. Fall-back still does not choose an instant. That is enough to ask what a selected range means, and not enough to create Protected Time, a Block, or a Commitment from it.
